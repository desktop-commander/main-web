The HEIC Problem

Apple switched to HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) as the default iPhone photo format in iOS 11. The format compresses better than JPG while maintaining quality—great for phone storage, frustrating for everything else.

You'll hit issues when:

  • Windows apps show blank thumbnails or won't open the files
  • Photo printing services reject uploads
  • You need to share photos with someone on an older system
  • Web forms only accept JPG or PNG

The format itself isn't going anywhere, so you need a conversion workflow.

Working with Large Photo Libraries

If you have thousands of HEIC files, a careful approach helps avoid surprises.

Test first. Convert a small batch and verify the quality before processing everything:

Work in chunks. Processing a year at a time is easier to verify than everything at once:

Convert HEIC files from January 2024 to JPG, then let me know when done

Preserve originals until you're sure. Don't delete anything until you've verified the conversions:

Convert to JPG but keep originals in a "To-Review" folder until I confirm everything looks right

Check disk space. Conversion creates duplicates temporarily. A folder with 50GB of HEIC files needs roughly 100GB free while both versions exist.

Quality Considerations

Converting HEIC to JPG involves some quality loss—JPG is a lossy format. For most purposes (sharing, printing, viewing on screens), the difference is imperceptible.

If you need lossless output:

Look for the file called 'filename' in my [folder]. Convert its HEIC format to PNG format. Keep the original file and create converted version. Open the folder with photos when you are done.

PNG preserves full quality but creates significantly larger files (3-5x the HEIC size). For archival purposes, you might keep the HEIC originals and only convert copies for specific uses.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do my photos get uploaded anywhere? ▾
No. With Desktop Commander, file conversion happens on your machine. While model providers process prompts, your actual photos and files are not uploaded or transmitted.
What happens to photo metadata (EXIF)? ▾
Date, location, and camera settings transfer to the converted files by default. If you want to strip metadata for privacy, you can ask: Convert to JPG and remove all EXIF data
Can I convert to other formats? ▾
Yes—PNG, TIFF, WebP, whatever your system supports. For example: Convert HEIC files to WebP format
How fast is conversion? ▾
It varies by hardware, but expect roughly 100 files per minute on a modern Mac. Larger batches run in the background while you continue working.
Can I undo if something goes wrong? ▾
Desktop Commander doesn't have built-in undo, which is why preserving originals until you verify the results is important. If you use Git with your photo folders (some people do), you have version control as a safety net.
